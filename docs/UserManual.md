Understanding Fund Tags and Restricted Money in Temper
Temper tracks restricted funds by letting you place a Fund tag on individual lines of a transaction. Not every line counts, however.
Important rule

Fund tags placed on asset accounts (such as the Checking account) are ignored.

Only fund tags on income, expense, or net-asset (equity) lines affect a fund’s balance.
This keeps the Checking account as a shared pool of cash while the restriction travels with the purpose of the money.
How to record common situations
Opening Balances

When you first bring a restricted fund onto the books, date the entry the day before your real transactions begin.

Debit the Checking account and credit Net Assets – With Donor Restrictions.

Put the Fund tag on the Net Assets line (not on Checking).

That single tag gives the restricted fund its correct starting balance.
Receiving a Restricted Contribution

Debit Checking and credit the appropriate income account (for example “Special Contributions” or “Insurance Recoveries”).

Place the Fund tag on the income line.

The restricted fund balance increases and the money is properly marked as restricted.
Spending from a Restricted Fund

Debit the normal expense account (Maintenance & Repairs, Program Supplies, etc.) and credit Checking.

Place the Fund tag on the expense line.

The restricted fund balance decreases by the amount spent.
Releasing Restrictions

When the donor’s purpose has been fulfilled, you reclassify the money from restricted to unrestricted:

Debit Net Assets – With Donor Restrictions and credit Net Assets – Without Donor Restrictions.

Put the Fund tag on the debit (With Donor Restrictions) line.

This reduces the restricted fund balance and moves the resources into unrestricted net assets.
Why this method is used

It avoids accidentally double-counting or zeroing a fund when both sides of a transaction are tagged. It also matches standard fund-accounting practice: the restriction lives with the reason for the transaction, not with the cash itself.
Following these patterns keeps fund balances accurate, reports clear, and the system consistent for every user.

Fund Tags, Fund Balances & Releases — How Temper Works
(Aligned with U.S. GAAP for not-for-profits, notably ASC 958 and ASU 2016-14, Presentation of Financial Statements of Not-for-Profit Entities)
Core rule
Fund tags on asset accounts (Checking, other cash/bank accounts, etc.) are ignored when calculating fund balances.

Only fund tags on non-asset lines affect fund balances:

Income
Expense
Net Assets (With Donor Restrictions / Without Donor Restrictions)

Cash remains a shared pool. Restrictions follow the economic purpose of the transaction, consistent with the principle that donor restrictions are reflected in net assets, not by segregating cash in the primary statements (ASU 2016-14).
How fund balances move

Tag on an Income or Net Assets – WDR credit line → increases that fund
Tag on an Expense debit line → decreases that fund
Tag on a Net Assets – WDR debit (release) → decreases that fund

Typical patterns

Restricted contribution

Debit Checking (no effective fund tag)

Credit Income (or Net Assets – WDR) with the fund tag
Restricted expense

Debit Expense with the fund tag

Credit Checking (no effective fund tag)
Opening restricted balance

Debit Checking

Credit Net Assets – WDR with the fund tag
Release from restrictions

Debit Net Assets – WDR with the fund tag

Credit Net Assets – WODRThis supports the required presentation of net assets with and without donor restrictions and the disclosure of releases when purpose restrictions are met (ASC 958-205 / ASU 2016-14).

Fund balances vs Net Assets – WDR

Fund balances are the day-to-day operational view of restricted resources (Helping Hands, Building Fund, etc.).
Net Assets – With Donor Restrictions (WDR) and Without Donor Restrictions (WODR) are the equity control accounts used for opening balances, formal releases, and audit integrity.
They do not need to match on every transaction. They are reconciled at period end.

Under ASU 2016-14, the primary statement presentation is by net asset class (with vs without donor restrictions), not by detailed internal funds. Temper’s named funds are an analytical management tool that rolls up into those classes.
Formal releases
Periodic releases (monthly, quarterly, or year-end) reclassify amounts from Net Assets – WDR to Net Assets – WODR when donor restrictions have been satisfied.

Their purpose is proper equity classification and auditability—not operational tracking of individual funds or cash.
Practical takeaway

Tag income and expense lines to manage restricted funds day to day.
Do not rely on fund tags on asset lines for balances.
Use Net Assets – WDR / WODR for structure, opening balances, and formal releases.
Reconcile the sum of fund balances to Net Assets – WDR when closing a period.

This keeps Temper consistent with the with/without donor restriction model required by U.S. GAAP for not-for-profit entities while remaining practical for day-to-day church use.
